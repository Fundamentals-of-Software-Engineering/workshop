# Research a Role with Gemini Notebook

The career lab. Dan does it live at the end of the career section. Follow along if you have a
Google account, or do it later on your own.

[Gemini Notebook](https://notebook.google.com) used to be called NotebookLM. Google renamed it in
July 2026. It's the same tool, so any guide that says NotebookLM still applies.

A general chatbot answers from everything it has ever read. Gemini Notebook answers from the sources
you give it, with citations. That makes it a good research partner. It also means your answers are
only as good as your sources.

## What you need

- A Google account. If your work account can't open Gemini Notebook, use a personal one.
- A browser

Don't upload anything private from work. Check your company's policy first.

## The lab

### Part 1: Add sources

1. Go to https://notebook.google.com and create a new notebook.
2. Add the sample sources from [`sources.md`](sources.md): four real Staff Software Engineer
   postings and two guides. Want a different role? Swap in three postings for that role instead.

### Part 2: Ask, then check

Ask the notebook:

> What skills show up in every posting? Which show up in only one?

Then:

> Based on these sources, what are the three skills I should build first, and how could I practice
> each one in the next three months?

For each answer, click one citation and read the source. Did the source really say that?

Pick one step you'll take next week.

## Discussion

Job postings are wish lists. How do you tell a must-have from a nice-to-have?

## Take it further

- **Ask more.** How many years of experience do the postings want? Which certifications come up?
  What does a normal week look like? What are the common interview questions?
- **Compare with a plain search.** Ask the same question in a search engine or a chatbot with no
  sources. Which answer could you check? Did either one make something up?
- **Update your personal tech radar.** See [`../tools/tech-radar-lab`](../tools/tech-radar-lab/).
- **Practice the interview questions out loud.** Don't let AI write your cover letter. It should
  sound like you.
- **Ask someone who has the job.** Buy them a coffee. No tool replaces that conversation.
