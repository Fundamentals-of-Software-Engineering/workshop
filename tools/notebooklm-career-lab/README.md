# Research a Role with NotebookLM

**Optional extra content.** Use it if there's time at the end of the day, or do it at home.

In July 2026, Google renamed NotebookLM to **Gemini Notebook**. It's at https://notebook.google.com
now. The old https://notebooklm.google.com address still works and takes you there. It's the same
tool, so any guide that says NotebookLM still applies.

Pick a role you might want in three to five years. Use an AI research tool to learn what it takes.
Then check what it told you.

A general chatbot answers from everything it has ever read. Gemini Notebook answers from the sources
you give it, with citations. That makes it a good research partner. It also means your answers are
only as good as your sources.

## What you need

- A Google account. If your work account can't open Gemini Notebook, use a personal one.
- A browser

Don't upload anything private from work. Check your company's policy first.

## The lab

### Part 1: Pick a role and gather sources

1. Pick a role. Some ideas: Staff Engineer, Solutions Architect, Engineering Manager, Developer
   Advocate, Site Reliability Engineer.
2. Go to https://notebook.google.com and create a new notebook.
3. Add four to six sources. Don't spend long here. Mix the types:
   - Two or three real job postings for the role. If a link won't import, paste the text into a
     Google Doc, or save the page as a PDF, and add that.
   - A career guide or a blog post by someone who has the job
   - A conference talk on YouTube
   - Your own notes, or your current job description

### Part 2: Research the role with your notebook

Ask the notebook questions like these. Change them to fit your role.

- What skills show up in every posting? Which show up in only one?
- How many years of experience do these postings ask for? How much do they agree?
- Which certifications or credentials come up? Do they look required or nice to have?
- What does a normal week look like in this role, based on these sources?
- What are the most common interview questions for this role? How should I answer them?
- Which books, podcasts or talks do these sources recommend?

For each answer, click one citation and read the source. Did the source really say that?

### Part 3: Compare with a plain search

Ask one of the same questions in a search engine or a general chatbot with no sources. Compare:

- Which answer was more specific?
- Which one could you check?
- Did either one make something up?

### Part 4: Make a plan

Ask:

> Based on these sources, what are the three skills I should build first, and how could I practice
> each one in the next three months?

Pick one step you'll take next week. Write it down.

## Discussion

Share with a neighbor:

- Which role did you pick? What surprised you?
- How did the research change compared to plain searching?
- Where did the tool help? Where did you still need your own judgment?
- Job postings are wish lists. How do you tell a must-have from a nice-to-have?

## Take it further

- Use the plan to update your personal tech radar. See `../tech-radar-lab`.
- Practice the interview questions out loud with a voice assistant. Don't let AI write your cover
  letter. It should sound like you.
- Ask someone who has the job. Buy them a coffee. No tool replaces that conversation.
